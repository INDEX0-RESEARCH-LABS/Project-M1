**Reading of the map:** the segment is split three ways — (1) large Tier-1s with magnet-free programmes (Sona, Bosch, Mahle, Nidec) that do not publish prices; (2) funded magnet-free startups (Chara, Conifer, Viridian, Sterling/AEM) selling design-plus-supply; (3) a long tail of traders reselling Chinese hub/BLDC motors at published asking prices. Only tier 3 has transparent pricing, and tier 3 sets the price ceiling every Indian design must beat.

## (c) White-space assessment

**1. The 0.5–1 kW band is not white space — it is the most commoditised corner of the market.**
A 1 kW, 48 V e-rickshaw motor retails in India at ₹3,200–4,900 (₹3,200–4,900/kW) and lands from China at ~₹2,749/kW FOB. At that price the entire motor BOM is a few hundred rupees of steel, copper, and magnet. Any new architecture at this power level is competing against a part that is already cheaper than the design effort required to replace it. The project's 0.5 kW/1 kW target sits here.

**2. Magnet-free is no longer white space either.**
Chara (SynRM, ₹52 cr Series A, 100k units/yr capacity, ~100 customers), Conifer (ferrite axial flux, $20 M seed, Pune line), Sona Comstar (ferrite-assisted SynRM validated for e-3W/LCV), Ola (ferrite, AIS-041-certified at 7 and 11 kW), Simple Energy (HRE-free, in production), and Sterling Gtake/AEM (HDRM, FY27 target) all occupy the space. A 2026 entrant cannot claim novelty on "no rare-earth magnets".

**3. The distinction that still has technical room: *magnet-free* vs *rare-earth-free*.**
Ola, Sona, Conifer, and Viridian all still use ferrite. Only Chara and a handful of SRM players eliminate magnets entirely. Ferrite is cheap and abundant but has ~1/3 the energy product of NdFeB, so ferrite-assisted designs pay in mass and volume. At equal envelope, a true SynRM pays in torque density — which is exactly the deficit the project's own D-012 audit measured (0.64 kPa certified gap shear vs ~2.5 kPa for a 2W-class machine). That is a physics problem, not a positioning problem.

**4. Where actual gaps remain (ranked by evidence strength):**
- **10–30 kW e-3W/LCV, Indian DVA-compliant.** Sona has *validated* a ferrite-assisted SynRM for LCV but has not announced production. PLI-Auto requires 50% domestic value addition. This is the one band where a validated, magnet-free, India-made design has no confirmed production incumbent. It is also capital-intensive to enter.
- **Integrated motor + controller + reduction for L5 cargo e-3W.** Tsuyo and others sell motors; controllers are usually separate or imported. System integration is a real gap, but it is an electronics/software gap, not a magnetics gap.
- **Design and validation IP, not parts.** Chara's moat is its design-plus-controls capability, not its factory. A certified simulation toolchain validated against Indian material grades and Indian drive cycles is saleable to OEMs and Tier-1s without a production line — and is the only wedge consistent with a zero-budget team.
- **Aftermarket replacement.** India's e-rickshaw fleet is large and motors fail; but this channel is the most price-sensitive of all and is served by the same ₹3,200 traders.

**Verdict.** The original premise — that a 0.5–1 kW magnet-free traction motor is an open market — does not survive the price data. That band is a commodity priced at ₹3,200–5,100/kW with a Chinese FOB floor near ₹2,749/kW, and the magnet-free claim is already held by funded, OEM-engaged competitors. The defensible residual positions are (i) 10–30 kW magnet-free LCV traction where validated designs exist but production supply does not, and (ii) design/validation IP sold as engineering rather than hardware. Both require a different business model than the one this project is currently scoped around.

## (d) Unknowns

1. **OEM transfer prices.** No Tier-1 contract price is public for any supplier in the map. The entire price table is marketplace asking prices. A ±30–50% gap to contract pricing is plausible but unmeasured.
2. **Whether the magnet-free programmes are shipping.** Sona says "developed, tested, validated". Ola says it "will begin integrating". Neither is a production-volume disclosure. Chara's 40,000-motors-by-FY27 target is a target.
3. **Efficiency and durability parity.** AIS-041 certifies net power only. No public third-party data compares ferrite/SynRM vs NdFeB PMSM efficiency maps, derating, or field failure rates at equal envelope.
4. **Ferrite supply chain.** Ferrite is abundant, but Indian ferrite magnet production capacity is not documented in this pass. "Magnet-free" removes the rare-earth risk; "ferrite-assisted" only moves it.
5. **Warranty cost.** OEM warranties run 3 yr/30,000–50,000 km (Ola 3/40,000; TVS iQube 3/50,000; Hero Vida motor/VCU 3/50,000; Ather 3/30,000). The field-failure cost of a new architecture is the single largest unquantified risk and is not public for any supplier.
6. **Import duty and DVA accounting.** Landed cost of the $29 FOB motor after duty, freight, and PLI DVA treatment is not computed here.
7. **Chara unit pricing and power ratings.** Chara's own product page redirected to an unrelated site during this pass; no first-party power/pricing data was obtained. All Chara claims above are from press coverage.
8. **Contract manufacturing cost.** Lamination and stamping quotes (MOQ 1,000 kg / 1,000 pcs, ₹65–150/kg) were noted in prior desk work but are not re-verified here and are marked **UNSOURCED**.
